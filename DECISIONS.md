# Decisions

Choices made where the spec was silent or ambiguous (spec instruction: pick the simplest option and record it here). Numbers referenced live in `src/data/balance.json` unless noted.

## Power is rarity, a smaller battle grid, x100 upgrades (Nov 3, newest)

Kevin's playtest notes, in order: "health bars are too cramped... everything's too close together for the setting... push the houses and stuff a bit further", "can u just shrink the characters a bit more", "make the upgrades x100 each time it goes up", "something cannot be more rare than something but have less power score", "if it has an enchantment, that makes it more rare. reflect that", "instead of power and rarity, they're just one filter of power. also have a insta equip biggest upgrade button", "instead of doing a decimal increase. put it as a percentage increase instead", "as long as the player drags the item up towards the top of the screen, it should land directly into the slot", "pixelated 5 and 2 look weird, fix". These rows replace the rows they mention.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Power is rarity. An item's power comes only from its "1 in X" odds, enchantments included: power = 30 x X^0.2 (`items.power` in balance.json), so a rarer item always has more power and an enchanted item outranks the same item without it (an enchanted Epic is rarer than a plain Legendary, so it is stronger). Its stats are sized to that power (main stats, substats and unique bonuses scale together). Item level and world no longer change an item. Replaces W4's per-item world tier and the item-level scaling of stats. | Kevin's rule. Sorting by power is now sorting by rarity, so the bag has one sort (Power). |
| Q2 | The world and level bonus moved to the raccoon: every worn item's own stats grow 6% per raccoon level, and its flat HP / ATK / DEF / regen grow 1.9x per world past the best one beaten (the old item-tier growth). Item tiles show power as worn (rarity power times that bonus), so they add up to the raccoon's Power. | Keeps progression through worlds (enemies still grow about 2.8x per world) without letting world or level break the rarity order. In the simulator the pace is close to before (see Balance). |
| Q3 | **Equip best** (Bag tab, and next to Equipped on the Roll tab) wears the biggest upgrade in every slot at once; it shows how many slots would change. | "Insta equip biggest upgrade button." |
| Q4 | Every leveled upgrade line costs 100x more per tier (it was 8 to 10x; the Extra Reel already was 100x). One-tier specials are unchanged. Replaces H4's growth. | Kevin's request. Upgrades become rarer milestones: in 2 hours a player buys fewer tiers, rolls about half as many items and finds fewer Legendaries, while the gear bonus (Q2) keeps world progress near the old pace. |
| Q5 | Bonuses read as percentages everywhere: "+15% luck" (not "+0.15"), "+5% spin speed", "+100% turbo speed", "+100% luck on lucky spins", and the Luck popup's sources. | Kevin's request. |
| Q6 | Drag to equip is forgiving: an item equips when it is let go anywhere above the menus (the battle view or the stat strip), on a gear panel, or after an upward drag of 180 stage pixels from wherever it started. | "As long as the player drags the item up... it should land directly into the slot." |
| Q7 | The battle grid is 10 screen pixels per art pixel (it was 12), so the view is 108 x 82 art pixels and every character and backdrop shows about 17% smaller, with more of the street in view. Fighters stand on row 67. Foes stand further apart, HP bars are slimmer, and only the foe being fought shows its HP number. Replaces P1's pixel size; everything in battle still shares one pixel size. | "Shrink the characters a bit more" and "health bars are too cramped", without breaking "make sure the pixel sizes and everything are consistent". |
| Q8 | The street is redrawn with more detail and more room: houses set back behind front yards and spread apart, textured roofs and walls, curtains and porch lights; the far town, sidewalk and foreground have texture too. The street tile is 228 art pixels wide (was 171); each street layer lives in its own file (`src/render/pixel/street/`). | "Make everything more detailed and nicer" and "push the houses and stuff a bit further". |
| Q9 | Pixelify Sans draws 2 like Z and 5 like S, so a tiny font, Dumpster Digits (`tools/fonts/digits.py`), redraws just those two on Pixelify Sans's own grid (a flat-topped 5 with a square corner, a round-topped 2 with a stepped diagonal); it is listed before Pixelify Sans in every font stack. | "Pixelated 5 and 2 look weird." |
| Q10 | Save version 7: every item's power is recomputed from its odds and its stats rescaled to match; a spin in flight is dropped. | |
| Q11 | World 5 "Autumn Avenue" became the farm world, "Funky Farmland": its own farm backdrop (barn, silo, fields, a dirt road; `src/render/pixel/farm.ts`, not graded) under the same golden harvest sky, with farm-animal enemies from the packs Kevin sent: Angry Hen, Quacker (mallard), Mad Bunny, Hog Hooligan, Ram Rager (black ram) and the boss Mega Moo (spotted cow, heavy hit "Udder Chaos"). They copy existing stat lines (pigeon, pigeon, rat, rat, punk raccoon; the boss copies Big Earl), so the world is as hard as before. The animals are one-frame stills: they bob and get knocked out of the park like the rats. | "Make this for a farmland world." World 5 is reached in the first half hour, and its harvest-sunset sky already fit a farm. |
| Q12 | The icon pack Kevin sent (MV Icons, 32 x 32) is used for themed world props, halved to the battle grid: snowmen, Christmas trees and presents (Snowy Summit, Glacier Gulch), lollipops, candy canes and a cupcake (Candy Corner), jack-o'-lanterns, tombstones, a ghost and a black cat (Haunted Hollow), cauldrons (Toxic Bayou), wheat, pumpkins and cabbage (Golden Gardens). They stand in the front yards on plain grass; jack-o'-lanterns and cauldrons glow at night. The rest of the sheet (letters, keyboard keys, logos, consoles, underwear, blocks) does not fit the game. | "See which ones u can use." Halving keeps one pixel size in battle. |

## Lit skies, atmosphere and a clearer battle view (Nov 3, late)

Kevin sent two pixel-art pictures (a sunset over a lake with fireflies; a moonlit garden with glowing lamps and string lights) and asked for "nicer shaders on the background and stuff like this", then: "make more space for the top section. it's way too cramped... see which things u can remove and just put somewhere else".

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | Backdrops are lit the way pixel artists paint light, on the art grid (`src/render/pixel/atmosphere.ts`): skies in color bands mixed in OKLab, their edges broken into short runs; a sun, moon or planet inside a halo of stepped rings, drawn over the clouds so they catch its glow; stars; clouds built from ellipses and shaded on the whole cloud's outline (rim, lit band, shaded band), puffy heaps by day and thin streaks lit from below at sunset. The street is lit per world: an ambient multiply (dusk and night darken and tint it), aerial haze on the far and mid layers (dusky silhouettes against a sunset), rim light on the skyline (a white rim gives the snowy worlds snow caps), lit windows and lamps glowing in stepped rings, and a vignette in stepped rings. | The reference pictures: banded gradients, haloed sun and moon, lit cloud edges, warm lights against cool shadows. |
| L2 | It is painted on the CPU into textures (1 texel per art pixel), not done with GPU shaders. Glow is added to the art and semi-transparent past it; every art pixel has one color and one alpha. | A smooth GPU blur or bloom would break the one-pixel-size rule (P1). This stays crisp, also works on the canvas renderer, and is deterministic and tested in Node. A world's art builds in about 20 ms, once per visit. |
| L3 | Each world's atmosphere is data: `look.atmo` in `worlds.json` (sky colors and horizon, sun / moon / planet, stars, clouds, haze, rim light, ambient color, share of windows lit, lamps, light color, particles, weather, vignette). The old `skyTop`, `skyBottom` and `night` fields are gone. Day worlds stay bright (Sunny Suburbs, Boardwalk Beach, Candy Corner, Snowy Summit); Pinecone Park, Autumn Avenue, Golden Gardens, Lava Landfill, Toxic Bayou and Mushroom Marsh are sunsets; Drizzle Downtown rains; the rest are night or storm (Moon Junkyard has the Earth in its sky, Cosmic Compactor a ringed planet). | Gives the 19 worlds that share the street their own mood. |
| L4 | Lights come from the scenery: windows are found in the house art (glass and muntin pixels closed in by frame), plus the far houses' one-pixel windows; two new street lamps stand behind the curb and throw a pool of light on the sidewalk (unlit by day); the dungeon gets four wall torches (drawn for the game) and its two fountains glow. Torches flicker in battle (animated flame and glow). | |
| L5 | Moving parts (`src/render/ambience.ts`), always on whole art pixels and seeded per world: twinkling and shooting stars, fireflies, leaves, petals, snow, rain (longer streaks in storms), embers, glowing spores, sparkles, wisps and dust motes; storms flash lightning with a bolt. Reduce motion hides the drifting particles and shooting stars; reduce flashing turns lightning off. | |
| L6 | Worlds tab thumbnails show each world lit as in battle. They are made in the background, one world per task, so the tab opens at once. `tools/worldPreview.ts` renders any worlds' backdrops to a PNG for art direction. | Making all 20 at once would freeze the tab for a moment on a phone. |
| L7 | The top of the battle view only holds the HUD row now. The world's name shows as a title card when you arrive, then fades. Wave progress moved to the bottom, next to the globe button (both open Worlds). The boss name and HP bar sit right under the HUD (they used to overlap the wave track). The luck potion timer moved into the stat strip's Luck cell, which already glows while boosted. The dev panel opens from Settings instead of a floating button. The "Drag gear onto me!" tip is smaller and sits just above the raccoon. | Kevin's request. |
| L8 | Leaving a world frees its textures, except that a foe still finishing its death animation keeps its texture until it is gone. | A dying foe could otherwise draw from a freed texture. |

## Kevin's raccoon, asset packs, one pixel size and a fantasy world (Nov 3, after the hex map)

Kevin sent his own raccoon sprite sheet ("here just use this"), then a series of asset packs: pixel clothes ("just make sure the pixel sizes and everything are consistent"), Tiny RPG Soldier & Orc, 0x72 DungeonTileset II, Super Pixel Effects Gigapack, Mucho Pixels Character Animation pack and a cozy UI kit, and asked for the fantasy characters to go in one world ("one of the worlds would be fantasy themed, just throw them in there"). These rows replace X1's pixel size and X2's raccoon. Licenses: `CREDITS.md`.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | *Pixel size superseded by Q7 (10 px).* Every pixel in the battle is the same size: 12 screen pixels per art pixel (the view is 90 x 68 art pixels). The hero, pack characters and effects are drawn 1:1; the street, rats, pigeons and the two raccoon bosses were redrawn at half resolution; item icons worn by the hero and the older still effects are shown at half resolution (each 2 x 2 block becomes its most common color, ties to the darker). Nothing in battle is scaled or squashed; only weapon swings and knock-outs rotate. | Kevin's raccoon is 15 x 15 pixels and the pack characters 16 to 30 tall: 12 px makes them readable on a phone, and halving the old art keeps one grid. |
| P2 | The hero is Kevin's sheet (6 x 6 frames of 24 x 24), copied 1:1: idle loop; walking is a hop (crouch, stretch-up and stand frames with a 2-pixel jump); a swing is the arms-out frame for the wind-up and the dash frame (with its dust cloud) for the strike; hurt shows the blushing frame; level-ups, boss wins and Epic-or-better equips play the cheer; a KO plays lie-down then the sleep loop (with its z's), and the next stage plays wake-up. | Use the sheet's own animations; it has no walk cycle, so a hop suits a front-facing raccoon. |
| P3 | Worn gear on the small raccoon: hats, masks, shields, backpacks and weapons are their item icons at half resolution, placed on landmarks of the standing pose; jackets, gloves, shoes and necklaces recolor the fur of that body part in the item's main color (dark, mid, light), baked into every frame. Gear follows the body by an offset per frame, found by matching the head's silhouette; curled-up frames carry no gear. Rings aren't shown. Weapons lean 45 degrees in their art except the upright umbrella. | Every item still shows, at the same pixel size as the raccoon, without drawing 80 worn sprites. |
| P4 | Pack art keeps its own colors: each sheet carries its own palette (`AnimSheet`, `src/render/pixel/indexed.ts`), generated 1:1 from the pack PNGs by `tools/packs/*.py` and cropped to its frames. The raw packs are not in the repository. | Recoloring pack art to the game palette would lose its shading; its colors already sit well together. |
| P5 | Enemies: Punk Raccoons are Kevin's raccoon in the world's fur colors with a mohawk in the accent color; Officer Dale is the Mucho Pixels man (white shirt recolored to the world's color) with idle, walk, punch, hurt and death; rats, pigeons, Big Earl and Vinnie are hand-drawn stills that bob. Animated enemies walk in, punch or swing when they attack, flinch when hit and play their death; stills are knocked out of the park as before. Enemies face left (art drawn facing right is mirrored). | Use the packs' animations where they exist; the punk raccoons now match the hero. |
| P6 | World 8 "Dusty Dunes" became the fantasy world, "Dungeon Dumps": a 0x72 dungeon hall backdrop (no sky, not color-graded), with Pumpkin Dude and Plague Doc (0x72), Tin Soldier and Orc Brute (Tiny RPG) and the boss Dumpgor the Demon (0x72 big demon). The new enemies copy existing stats (pigeon, rat, rat, punk raccoon; the boss copies Big Earl, heavy hit renamed "Hellfire Stomp"), so the world is as hard as before: the simulator still clears World 8 between Worlds 7 and 9 at the same pace. | Kevin's request. World 8 is reachable mid-game, and Dusty Dunes was the least distinct of the reskinned streets. Worlds can now choose a backdrop (`look.scenery`). |
| P7 | Effects come from the Super Pixel Effects Gigapack (small variants, 1:1): a small spark on every blow, an explosion ring plus an impact spray on crits, an explosion on boss kills and splash damage, a lightning burst on chain lightning, a sparkle burst on Epic-or-better equips, luck potions (green) and wave heals (green), and a firework on level-up. The pack's coin burst and fire are not used (64 x 56 and 38 x 54: bigger than most enemies at this pixel size); burns and flying coins stay small pixel sprites. Sparks, dust and confetti are 1 to 3 pixel squares. The credit "Pixel Art Assets - Will Tice / unTied Games" is shown in Settings. | The pack's license requires the credit. |
| P8 | The clothes pack became 21 items (see I13); the cozy UI kit is not used yet. | |

## Pixel art, reels and Extra Reels (Nov 3)

Kevin asked for the whole game in pixel art, for rolling to look like items scrolling down until one lands (not a wheel), for the items rolling past to be blacked out but show their odds, leaning rarer than the real odds to entice, and for expensive upgrades that add more reels. These rows replace V1's wheel and R14's cel-shaded look.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| X1 | *Pixel size superseded by P1.* All art is pixel art drawn in code from one 39-color palette, as rows of palette characters (`src/render/pixel/`, rules in `docs/PIXEL_ART.md`). The battle is drawn at 6 screen pixels per art pixel with nearest-neighbor scaling (the view is 180 x 137 art pixels); weapons rotate during swings, but the hero and enemies no longer stretch or tilt. | "Make the game all pixel art." |
| X2 | *Superseded by P2 and P3.* The raccoon copies Kevin's reference picture (a pair of low-res pixel raccoons, one drawn here): one blob for head and body, square ears with dark insides, cream brows, a dark mask with shiny black eyes, a cream muzzle with a black nose, flat tan fur with darker sides, stubby feet and a ringed tail. He is drawn in a coarse 30 x 23 grid with each of his pixels 2 art pixels wide, so he is as chunky as the reference; he is tan so he stands apart from the gray punk raccoons. Each of the 63 base items has its own 16 x 16 icon in its natural colors; rarity shows on the tile around it. On the raccoon, hats, shields, backpacks and weapons reuse the icons; masks, jackets, shoes, necklaces and gloves have worn versions fitted to the body. Rings are too small to show. | One look per item instead of one per slot. |
| X3 | The UI uses Pixelify Sans, square corners everywhere, pixel borders on buttons and cards, and flat color bands instead of gradients. | Match the pixel art. |
| X4 | The wheel is gone. Each reel is a vertical strip that rolls downward, slows, and stops its result under a gold win line, with a small clunk. While rolling, every item is a black silhouette with its "1 in X" odds showing in its rarity color; the result flashes into color as its reel stops. | "I do not mean an actual wheel... rolling downwards until it lands on something." |
| X5 | The items rolling past are a tease, not a sample: their rarities are weighted Junk 12, Decent 16, Shiny 18, Epic 20, Legendary 18, Mythic 10, Mint in Box 6 (the real odds are 1 in 3, 12, 80, 1,000, 25,000 and 1,000,000), 30% of the Epic-or-better ones show enchanted odds, and next to 65% of results below Legendary sits a Legendary-or-better near miss. Only the landed results come from the real odds, which the Luck popup lists. Every spin is free; per the Dopamine Playbook, this tease must never be used on a spin bought with real money. | "It shouldn't be faithful... more rare things in the mystery items to entice the player." |
| X6 | Extra Reel: a gold line on the upgrade map next to Luck I, 4 tiers, each adding a reel (up to 5). It costs 250 million gold, then 100 times more per tier. Each reel rolls its own item; a lucky or golden spin covers every reel; bad-luck protection triggers once per streak, not once per reel. Reels stop left to right, but an Epic-or-better result always stops last. The big "last roll" card shows the spin's best result. | "Upgrades that add how many things are rolling at the same time, but expensive." Cheaper reels (1 million) doubled the pace in the simulator. |
| X7 | Save version 6: the spin state holds one result per reel; nothing is in flight after loading. | |

## Wheel, drag-to-equip and the upgrade tree (Nov 3)

Kevin asked for a real spinning wheel instead of nonstop rolling, Scrap All and sort-by-rarity in the bag, a big "1 in X" on every item, no auto-equip (drag items onto the raccoon instead), Roblox-style upgrade costs (easy at first, then exponential unless you rebirth), a rebirth that actually wipes the raccoon, and a branching upgrade tree. These rows replace the rows they mention.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | *The wheel is replaced by reels (X4).* Every roll is one spin of a prize wheel. The result is decided with the seeded Rng when the spin starts; the wheel spins for 2.0 s, lands on it, rests 0.6 s, and the next spin starts on its own. One cycle is 2.6 s. Each Spin Speed level makes it 6% faster (compounding), down to a 0.7 s floor (0.75 s at the max level, 20). | "It should actually have a spinning wheel animation that you can speed up through upgrades. The base should be 2-3 seconds." |
| V2 | Trash Bags won in fights are spent one per spin as turbo spins: 2x as fast (Turbo Power raises it to 4x), never under 0.45 s per cycle. The wheel's 12 wedges show the target item plus 11 fillers drawn from your real current odds; the animation never changes the result. For Legendary and better, the build-up (glow, rattle, riser, silence drop) plays during the spin, and the reveal fires as it lands. | One roll is one visible spin, and bags still feel like a boost. |
| V3 | Nothing is equipped automatically. Every roll goes into the bag (when the bag is full, the weakest unlocked item, or the new roll if it is weaker still, is scrapped, as in R5). To equip, drag an item onto the raccoon or the gear row: with a mouse, press and drag; on touch, hold for 0.17 s, then drag (a quick swipe still scrolls). Items that would raise their slot's power get a green ▲ badge, and the Bag tab gets a dot. There is no "Equip best" button (Kevin withdrew that request). | "It shouldn't even auto equip. They have to drag it on themselves." |
| V4 | The bag sorts by Power or by Rarity (rarest first, then power); the choice is remembered on the device. Scrap All scraps every unlocked bag item at once for the usual scrap gold and XP; it takes two taps and shows the gold first. Equipped and locked items are never scrapped. | "There should be a Scrap All button, and also a sort by rarity button." |
| V5 | Every item shows "1 in X": the chance of rolling its rarity at base luck (x1), times the chance of its enchantments (how many, and which tiers) at the base enchant chance. It is a tilted banner over every item tile and a giant number in the reveal, the last-roll card and the item card. Epic and up pulse, Legendary shimmers gold, Mythic cycles through a rainbow, and Mint in Box shimmers pearl. Base luck is used so the number says how rare the item is, not how lucky your build is; the Luck popup still lists your real current odds. | "All items should show a 1 in ___", "VERY much exaggerated and highlighted". |
| V6 | *Superseded by H1 to H4.* Upgrades are a tree of 22 nodes branching from Luck: a Spin branch (Spin Speed, Bag Finder, Turbo Power, Lucky Spins, Lucky Streak, Golden Spin), an Enchanting branch (Enchant Mastery, Pity Shield), a Gold branch (Bag Space, Scrap Value, XP Boost, Potion Time, Offline Earnings) and a Strength branch (Toughness, Lifesteal, Crit Chance, Crit Damage, Boss Damage). A node unlocks when its parent reaches a set level (1 to 4). Each level costs base x growth^level, with growth from 1.75 to 3.0, so the first levels are cheap and later ones get exponentially pricier. | "More interesting upgrades, branching out like in Roblox." |
| V7 | *Amounts per tier changed by H3.* New effects: Golden Spin gives each spin a 1% chance per level (up to 10%) of x5 luck; Lucky Streak adds +0.25 per level to Lucky Spins' x2; Enchant Mastery moves enchantment odds from tier I toward tiers II and III; Pity Shield starts bad-luck protection 4 spins sooner per level (40 down to 20); Potion Time adds 10 s per level to Luck Potions; Scrap Value, XP Boost and Offline Earnings add percentages; Lifesteal, Crit Chance, Crit Damage and Boss Damage add flat stat points. | |
| V8 | Rebirth wipes the raccoon: equipped items, the bag, level and XP, gold, upgrades and world progress. It keeps Gems, the Codex, Dumpster Level and your best-find records. Each rebirth gives luck x(1 + 0.25 per rebirth) (was +0.15), gold x(1 + 0.5 per rebirth) (was +0.2), +10% ATK and +10% HP per rebirth, and makes every upgrade 10% cheaper (compounding). Requirements are unchanged from W8. | "The raccoon kept all his stats and items after rebirth, fix that", and "exponentially harder unless the player rebirths". The bigger multipliers make up for the wipe: in the simulator, every cycle reaches a new best world. |
| V9 | Save version 4: the roll timer becomes the spin state (no spin is in progress after loading), and the auto-equip setting is dropped. | |

## Worlds (Nov 2, later): 20 worlds of rising difficulty

Kevin asked for about 20 worlds, all open from the start: the hard ones one-shot you, but they pay proportionally more, so a lucky roll lets you jump ahead. These rows replace the stage rows they mention.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| W1 | The 20 Suburbs stages became 20 worlds (`worlds.json`). A world is 4 regular waves, then its boss; while farming, the world loops. | Kevin's request; one difficulty step per world. |
| W2 | Every world is open from the start. The Worlds tab (and the globe button on the battle) lists all 20 with a threat read-out: Easy, Tough, Deadly, or ONE-SHOT. The read-out comes from real combat math against your current stats, including the boss: ONE-SHOT means one regular hit is lethal; otherwise a margin compares (your HP x DPS) to theirs. Deadly and ONE-SHOT trips need a second tap. | "Access to all of them, but literally get one-shot on the difficult ones." |
| W3 | Per world, enemy HP grows x3.0, ATK x2.6 and DEF x2.6. The DEF constant in the damage formula grows x2.6 too, so DEF keeps the same meaning in every world. Gold grows x3.0 per world (in step with enemy HP, so pay is proportional to difficulty); XP grows x1.35. | "They also pay proportionately more." |
| W4 | *Superseded by Q1 and Q2 (the world bonus is on the raccoon now; the tier only sets scrap gold).* Items carry a tier: the world they were rolled in, capped at one past the best world beaten (so a suicide trip to World 20 can't farm World 20 gear). An item's flat HP/ATK/DEF/REGEN are multiplied by 1.9^(tier - 1); percent stats are not. Every slot now has a flat stat too, so every item's power grows with its world. | Better worlds drop better gear, the RNG-game loop. |
| W5 | Items grow slower than enemies (x1.9 against roughly x2.8 per world), so each world asks for better rarities, levels or upgrades than the last. Walls are broken by luck: a rare roll can carry you several worlds ahead. | "If the player gets lucky and rolls a really good item, they can advance." Without the gap, progress ran away (simulator: World 20 in 21 minutes). |
| W6 | A failed push falls back to the world it started from; a death in a world already beaten steps down one world, and each further death steps down again. A pushing raccoon that clears a world moves on to the next; a farming one retries the next unbeaten world once it is stronger than when it last died there. A "World N" button offers the next unbeaten world at any time. | |
| W7 | A world's first clear pays 10 + 5 x world Gems and a Luck Potion. Bosses pay 15x a regular kill's gold, 4x its XP, 3 Trash Bags, and drop a Luck Potion 15% of the time. | Bosses come every loop now, so potions can't be guaranteed. |
| W8 | *Rewards updated by V8.* Rebirth needs World 5 beaten (+2 worlds per rebirth, up to 20) and 3,000 x 3^(world - 1) x 4^rebirths gold. Luck per rebirth is +0.15 (was +0.25). Rebirth resets world progress. | Keeps rebirths from snowballing (simulator: hundreds per hour before this). |
| W9 | Rarities are rarer: Legendary 1 in 1,000, Mythic 1 in 25,000, Mint in Box 1 in 1,000,000 (Decent 3, Shiny 12, Epic 80). Rarity stat multipliers are wider: Junk 1, Decent 1.4, Shiny 2, Epic 3, Legendary 4.8, Mythic 8, Mint 14. Item level adds 6% per level (was 9%). Scrap XP no longer grows with the world. | Luck should decide progress, and big reveals should stay rare. |
| W10 | Save version 3: old stages map to worlds at 5 stages per world, existing items become World 1 gear, and the neighborhood bonuses are gone. | |

## Redesign (Nov 2): always-rolling RNG loop

Kevin asked for a Roblox-style rolling game: the dumpster rolls on its own, items carry a power number, a limited bag keeps the best ones, extra luck sources, an upgrade grid, rebirths that raise luck, and enchantments. These rows replace the older ones marked *superseded* below.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | *Superseded by V1 and V2.* No dive button. A free roll happens every 2.0 s (the Roll Speed upgrade brings it down to 0.4 s), and Trash Bags won in fights are spent automatically as bonus rolls, 3 per second. | "Constantly rolling without having to click." |
| R2 | *Odds updated by W9.* Rarities are "1 in X" chances: Decent 3, Shiny 10, Epic 50, Legendary 400, Mythic 5,000, Mint in Box 100,000. A roll tries the rarest tier first, then each lower tier, and each try succeeds with chance min(1, luck / X). The Luck popup lists the exact per-roll chances, computed by the same function the roller uses. | The RNG-game convention. The odds shown are always the real ones (playbook). |
| R3 | Luck = (1 + Luck upgrades + 0.05 per Dumpster Level above 1 + Lucky enchantments) x rebirth multiplier x Luck Potion. Bad-luck protection is unchanged: from Dumpster Level 3, after 40 rolls with nothing Epic or better, the next roll is guaranteed Epic or better. | Dumpster upgrades still help, plus several new luck sources. |
| R4 | Every 10th roll is a Lucky Roll at x2 luck, shown on a meter. The Lucky Rolls upgrade lowers 10 to as few as 4. | A visible "something good is coming" beat between rare pulls. |
| R5 | *Auto-equip removed by V3; the bag rules still apply.* Items have a power number. A roll is equipped automatically when it beats the equipped item's power (Auto-equip can be switched off in the Bag); otherwise it goes in the bag. The bag holds 20 items (+2 per Bag Space level), sorted by power. When it is full, the weakest unlocked item, or the new roll if it is weaker still, is scrapped for gold and XP. Locked items are never scrapped automatically. | "Keeps the highest-rated items." |
| R6 | Scrapping pays half the old recycle coins (times gold multipliers) and a quarter of the XP. Enchanted items pay +50% per enchantment tier. | Constant rolling would otherwise flood the economy. |
| R7 | *Superseded by V6.* The upgrade grid costs gold: Luck, Gold Gain, Roll Speed, Bag Space, Lucky Rolls, Enchanting, Strength (ATK%), Toughness (HP%), Bag Finder. Cost = base x growth^level, with a level cap per upgrade. Holding the buy button keeps buying. Upgrades reset on rebirth. | |
| R8 | *Superseded by W8 and V8.* Rebirth needs stage 10 cleared (+5 stages per rebirth, capped at the last stage) and 5,000 gold in hand (x4 per rebirth). Each rebirth gives luck x(1 + 0.25 per rebirth) and gold x(1 + 0.2 per rebirth), and resets stage progress, gold and upgrades. Items, the bag, player level, Dumpster Level, the Codex and Gems are kept. It takes two taps to confirm. | Keeping the bag and level makes rebirths feel like a boost instead of a loss. |
| R9 | Enchantments: each roll has a 5% chance (+1.5% per Enchanting level) of 1 to 3 enchantments (80/17/3%), each of tier I, II or III (70/25/5%). Every tier adds 15/30/55% item power. | R10 | Luck Potions give x2 luck for 60 s. One drops from every mini-boss and boss kill, and there is a 0.5% chance per regular wave. A new potion adds its time to one that is still running. | One more luck source, tied to fights. |
| R11 | Enemies got x2.4 HP and x1.7 ATK. | With a constant stream of gear, fights were over too fast to read and stages fell too quickly (simulator). |
| R12 | Save version 2. The migration moves an undecided dive item into the bag, adds the enchantment and lock fields, and drops Auto-Dive. | |

## Balance

The simulator (`npm run sim`) plays the real core with a bot that lets the reels roll, equips any bag item that beats what it wears (a simulated drag), lets the battle push through worlds on its own, buys the cheapest open hex it can afford every second (Extra Reels included), upgrades the dumpster when it can, rebirths as soon as it is allowed, and every 10 seconds jumps to the hardest world that reads Easy. With power-is-rarity and the 100x upgrade growth (Q1 to Q4), across 3 seeds over 2 hours:

- World 2 beaten at about 3 minutes, World 3 at 5 to 8, World 4 at 9 to 14, World 5 at 14 to 24. Each world takes longer than the last.
- Rebirths at about 14 to 24, 29 to 53, 62 to 76 and 80 to 112 minutes (4 or 5 in 2 hours); each cycle reaches further, and the best world after 2 hours is 11 to 14 (it was about 15 with 8 to 10x upgrade growth).
- Per 2 hours: about 5,000 to 6,000 items rolled (fewer Spin Speed tiers are bought), 50 to 60 Legendaries, 0 to 6 Mythics, no Mint in Box.

Earlier results (the tap-to-dive version, the single-neighborhood roller, the nonstop roller, the small-level upgrade tree and the single reel) are in the git history.
