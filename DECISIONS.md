## Recorded sounds (Nov 3, newest)

Kevin sent WAVs for sounds on the sound list (a page with every sound in the game, its file name and a button to hear it), one message per sound: "play sounds", "button click", "padlock tap", "these together, is the lock breaks", "reel stop", "rare crawl", "luck token, these three audios stacked. increase pitch each time it goes up", "glow climbs, increase pitch as it goes", "for the shake", "riser long", "riser short", "long jingle", "short jingle", "heartbeat, feel free to speed up etc etc", "these stacked together for slam"; and "reel tick is good, make it a bit louder", then "replace current reel stops with this" (a cash register ding for the level-up chime) and "these three together for when u sell like a big haul".

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | A sound with a recording (src/assets/sounds/<name>.mp3) plays it in place of its synthesized version; every other sound is still synthesized. The names are the sound list's: title_start (PLAY), tap (every button), lock_tap and lock_break (the padlocked dumpster), reel_land (each reel's stop), reel_creep (a rare crawl), token (luck token), glow_up (the build-up's glow climbing), rattle (the build-up's shake), riser_long and riser_short, jingle and jingle_long, heartbeat (the reveal's dark), slam (the reveal's number landing; gear from Limited Edition up slams onto the raccoon with it too, 1.5 dB louder per size up), haul (a big Scrap All, the same 1.5 dB per size). | Kevin's sounds, one at a time, without losing the rest. |
| S2 | tools/sounds.mjs makes the files with ffmpeg: several WAVs layered from the same start (the lock break's chain and stone smash; the token's three; the slam's impact, whoosh and chime, its tail cut at 5.5 s; the haul's register ding, coins and collect sparkle), silence trimmed from both ends, 48 kHz MP3 (VBR, about 190 kbps), layers that sum past full scale brought down to -1 dBFS. `--start` cuts a slow swell off the front (the reel stop's ding, 0.04 s, so it lands with the reel); `--last` keeps a long riser's end (the game only plays the end); `--length` keeps one lub-dub of the heartbeat loop. ## A locked start, one feature at a time, and a cleaner Roll tab (Nov 3)

Kevin: "get rid of rarest ever, clean up, things that don't need to be there get removed. for the tutorial, have everything locked until the tutorial walks the player through it. when they first start playing, they have to click the spinner for the lock and metal to fall off and then it starts spinning".

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | A new game's dumpster starts padlocked under two riveted steel plates and a strap (CSS on the pixel grid; the padlock is a 24 x 35 sprite, closed and popped open). The tutorial's first bubble says to tap it, and the hand taps the padlock. A tap clanks it; the shackle pops with sparks; the steel and the lock drop off the machine (whole pixels, their own gravity) with a crash; and the reels start, the first spin being the tutorial's Traffic Cone as before. In core the reels don't turn until `openDumpster` (save field `features`). | Kevin's request. |
| L2 | Everything else starts locked and opens when it becomes useful (core/systems/features.ts): the Bag once the first item is on; Luck (the luck badge, lucky-spin meter and bad-luck counter) and the Codex once the tutorial is over; Upgrades, and the dumpster's own upgrade (with its level and turbo-spin count), once the tutorial is over and one is affordable; Worlds after World 1's boss; Rebirth two worlds before the first rebirth's (World 3); Heists with the first rebirth (K24). "The tutorial is over" also comes after 40 rolls (`tutorial.overAtRolls`), so an unfinished tutorial never holds anything back. Core never blocks an action for a locked feature; the screens just don't offer it. Saves from before this have everything. | "Have everything locked until the tutorial walks the player through it." |
| L3 | Opened features are introduced one at a time, in that order. A gold "NEW! ..." bubble says what it is, its tab or button blinks a gold outline, and the hand points at it until it is tapped (or after 30 s for a tab, 12 to 15 s for a button), then the next one gets its turn; until its turn a feature stays locked on screen. A locked tab is faded with a lock and shakes its head when tapped ("Keep playing to unlock this!"); when it opens, its lock falls off with a chime. The tutorial's own steps come first (the Bag opens during the Equip best step, whose hand taps the Bag tab anyway). | One new thing at a time, each pointed out. |
| L4 | The Roll tab loses what it didn't need: the "rarest roll ever" line and the NEW BEST chip, the AUTO and seconds-per-spin label, and the "In your bag" chip (Scrapped still shows). Toasts that repeated what the screen already shows are gone: "Equipped X!" (the item's flight and the power count say it) and "Level up!" (the battle's LEVEL UP and the badge); the Codex bonus toast waits until the Codex has been introduced. | "Get rid of rarest ever, clean up, things that don't need to be there get removed." |
| L5 | Settings has Start a new game (a second tap confirms; it erases the save and goes back to the title). | Without it an existing save, which has everything open, could never show the new start. |
| L6 | The balance simulator taps the padlock at once; tests start from an opened dumpster unless they test the lock. | |

## Offline progress

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| O1 | The rate is tracked in per-minute buckets from regular kills and regular wave bags over the last 10 minutes of play. Boss bags and coin bursts are left out so one boss kill does not inflate a whole night. | |
| O2 | With less than 1 minute of data, the rate is estimated from the current stage: 5 waves x 4 enemies per 75 seconds. | "Estimate it from the stage's expected clear time." |
| O3 | The rate resets on every resume. Autosave snapshots `lastActiveAt` and the rate, so an app that is killed without a pause event still earns from its last save. | |
| O4 | The popup only appears if you were away at least 60 seconds and earned something. Uncollected rewards stack. Offline XP is paid too. | |
| O5 | Clock guard: if `now < lastActiveAt` on resume, grant nothing and reset `lastActiveAt` (as specified). While playing, `lastActiveAt` only moves forward. A server time check comes with M7. | The spec's reset still allows one capped 8 h exploit per clock rewind; the cap limits it. |
| O6 | A browser frame gap over 5 seconds with no visibility event is treated as offline time. | Phones freeze tabs without always firing events. |
| O7 | On load, the battle restarts at the start of the current stage. | Avoids restoring a half-finished fight. |

## Balance

The simulator (`npm run sim`) plays the real core with a bot that lets the reels roll, equips any bag item that beats what it wears (a simulated drag), lets the battle push through worlds on its own, taps Next 1.5 seconds after a world boss falls, buys the cheapest open hex it can afford every second (Extra Reels included), upgrades the dumpster when it can, rebirths as soon as it is allowed, and every 10 seconds jumps to the hardest world that reads Easy. With the slower climb (S1), the boss choice (S5), luck tokens (T5) and rebirth unlocks (T8), across 3 seeds over 2 hours:

- World 2 beaten at 6.5 to 8.5 minutes, World 3 at 16 to 22, World 4 at 26 to 36, World 5 at 35 to 56, World 6 at 78 to 98 and World 7 at 85 to 106. Each world takes longer than the last.
- The first rebirth comes at 35 to 56 minutes and the second at 85 to 106; the best world after 2 hours is 7.
- Per 2 hours: about 3,500 items rolled, 18 to 22 Legendaries, 1 to 3 Mythics and now and then a Vintage. Tokens added about 20% luck on average without moving the pace (before them: first rebirth at 30 to 56 minutes, second at 84 to 111).
- Unlocks don't change power (power is rarity), only which items turn up.

Earlier results (the tap-to-dive version, the single-neighborhood roller, the nonstop roller, the small-level upgrade tree and the single reel) are in the git history.
